Practice for cost-effectiveness work tests

Build the model against the clock. Get scored on the mistakes graders look for.

Each case gives you a brief and a messy data pack and starts a timer. You build a back-of-the-envelope cost-effectiveness model in Excel or Google Sheets and hand in the file. Claude grades it from 1 to 100 against written anchors and tells you the one fix that would raise your score most.

What it trains

  • Counterfactuals and attribution. Measuring against what would have happened anyway, and sharing credit with other actors.
  • Model mechanics. The right base, every cost, consistent units, and each discount applied once.
  • The decision and its crux. Whether the bar is cleared, and the value at which the answer flips.
  • Legibility under time pressure. Flagged assumptions and cited inputs that a teammate can audit in five minutes.

How it works

  1. Starting the clock shows the brief and the data. Reading counts toward the time, as it does in a real work test.
  2. Build your model in Excel or Google Sheets. In Sheets, use File → Download → Microsoft Excel (.xlsx).
  3. Upload the file and hand it in. Claude grades it from 1 to 100 against the anchors below, using your own Anthropic API key, or in a Claude chat if you don't have one.
